4.1 Condition 1 — Checklist decidability
Item 1 is the decisive lexical failure.
The actual Gate approves the B-leaning hybrid (TCD-IG). Design B proposes TCD-IG, while Design A proposes an age/activity/confidence architecture. Neither independent document proposes the final combined design as an already-reconciled candidate.
The hybrid recommendation is made in CROSS_REVIEW §5, retaining Design A's diagnostic and data-quality strengths while adopting Design B's primary measurement construct.
That distinction matters: the presence of TCD-IG in Design B does not establish that the other design has already been evaluated and reconciled.
Independent semantic classification challenges
I agree with the scorer's mechanical keyword results, but two items warrant stricter semantic treatment:
Item	Scorer class	Builder semantic assessment
4 — Snapshot as-of T	Pre-review	Synthesis-dependent selection. A and B discuss frozen T, and D identifies the snapshot as a feasible bounded case. The definite selection of snapshot-aligned first-pass analysis appears in the reconciliation and approval process.
9 — Date parse and timezone rule	Pre-review	Requirement identified, operational rule unresolved in the supplied text. The dossier requires a locked parser and timezone but does not itself supply the exact parsing expression. Cross-review item 9 also requests that rule rather than providing a complete implementation.
These are semantic qualifications, not changes to the frozen scorer or its mechanical counts.
Other pre-review items remain mechanically recognizable but require the synthesis or owner decision to acquire final authority. In particular:
- Item 2: Design B contains κ, but Design A proposes a different knob.
- Item 7: Design B proposes eligibility restrictions; the dossier warns that treatment needs locking.
- Item 8: Orphan-parent handling is proposed, not independently owner-approved.
- Item 12: The F01–F14 outline exists in B, while Design A proposes a less developed fixture set.
- Item 14: A and B differ on what mechanical aggregation SQL may perform.
The evidence therefore supports a narrower interpretation of pre-review: the component is mentioned or proposed before reconciliation, not necessarily that a reconciled decision has already been established.

The pre-review Design A contains four broad fixture categories: clear escalation, clear standard, ambiguous, and boundary cases. Design B defines a specific F01–F14 outline with expected classifications and design implications. The reconciled lock adopts the F01–F14 outline.
Of particular importance is F03:
Fixture	Construct test	Expected under Design B
F03	Type peer P75 = 90 days; dormancy = 100 days; κ = 2	STANDARD
For F03, the threshold is 2 × 90 = 180 days, while observed dormancy in the synthetic case is 100 days. Its expected STANDARD outcome follows arithmetically from the proposed type-conditional rule.
F03 separates the two measurement interpretations: a simplistic calendar-age approach could elevate a historically long-cycle request that the type-relative dormancy method does not.
This is textual fixture evidence, not a new fixture PASS.

The strongest semantic risk is not a missing field token. It is premature authorization of a measurement construct before the competing constructs have been reconciled.
Cross-review also preserves positive contributions from both designs. Design B supplies the operational dormancy and integrity method; Design A supplies diagnostic segmentation, data-quality checks, and clearer documentation of exclusions and output reasoning. The locked design combines these selectively.
A gate that simply chooses raw Design B early is not automatically equivalent to approving that reconciled hybrid.
6.2 Scope and validity limitations
The replay is deliberately limited.
The original Chicago baseline involved a coordinator and additional reviews. It is therefore not a perfectly clean experimental comparison. The variant was evaluated against preserved documents, not through a real owner approval exercise.
Consequently, the packet can establish which specified information was or was not available at the earlier stage. It cannot directly establish what a human owner would have approved under the counterfactual sequence.
Likewise, the token comparison demonstrates lexical coverage, not semantic equivalence. A shared token may be used under incompatible hypotheses, different rule orderings, or distinct authority assumptions.

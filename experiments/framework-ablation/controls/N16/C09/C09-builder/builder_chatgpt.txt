6. Semantic/regression risks and unchanged surfaces
6.1 Substantive versus process classification
A012 is a process review, but the frozen scorer treats it as substantive because the severity text contains lowercase process.
The immediate P1 count is unaffected because A012 has no later-ruling marker.
However, this exposes a fragile classification mechanism. A future record with different capitalization or wording could change the reported substantive population without a corresponding change in underlying ambiguity.
This report preserves the frozen calculation and separately discloses the issue.
6.2 Topic matching versus actual requirement anticipation
The 8/16 checklist statistic is susceptible to overinterpretation.
The matching expressions operate on short topic tokens rather than complete propositions.
Examples include:
- INCONCLUSIVE standing in for a specific status/date integrity rule.
- Eligible standing in for duplicate and legacy exclusions.
- SLA standing in for empirical peer-P75 labeling.
- Grok standing in for acceptance of a different model execution mode.
Consequently, the statistic measures broad lexical overlap, not complete pre-identification of eight later design requirements.
The frozen predicate correctly excludes this statistic from its success condition.
6.3 Retrospective classification of later rulings
The scorer identifies later-ruling requirements from Resolution and Severity text using terms including OPEN, Stage 3, later, and Deferred.
This provides reproducibility but does not distinguish between:
- An ambiguity requiring a stakeholder ruling.
- A technical detail intentionally delegated to measurement design.
- A documented follow-up.
- An unresolved governance or execution process item.
A003, A007, and A008 clearly identify meaningful downstream definition work.
A006 and A011 also refer to matters left for later consideration, but their presence does not independently prove a particular causal contribution from the pre-lock attack.
The distinction matters when interpreting the ablation beyond its frozen mechanical predicate.
6.4 Historical evidence is not a clean no-attack control
The SPEC discloses that the historical Chicago baseline involved coordinator intervention and additional reviews.
Therefore, the historical ledger reflects a combined decision-development process.
It is impossible from this replay alone to isolate precisely what would have happened had the ambiguity attack been removed while every other component remained unchanged.
This is a design limitation of the selected retrospective experiment, not evidence of a new execution failure.
6.5 Review keyword counts have limited semantic value
The four historical independent reviews supply meaningful written criticism.
However, counting occurrences of Blocking, Material, Revise, Tighten, or required revision does not measure the quantity, severity, independence, or successful resolution of substantive objections.
The review texts support qualitative analysis; the keyword count is only a mechanical descriptive statistic.

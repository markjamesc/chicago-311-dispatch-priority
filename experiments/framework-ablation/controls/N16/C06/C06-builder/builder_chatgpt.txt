2.3 Detection implementation
The frozen scorer uses two relevant detection patterns:
P_UP = re.compile(    r"(?:assign(?:s|ed|ing)?|action|label(?:s|led|ed)?|classif\w+)\b[^\n.|]{0,80}?\b([A-Z][A-Z_]{4,})\b")P_LO = re.compile(    r"\bassign\w*\b[^.\n]{0,60}?\b([a-z]+(?:-[a-z]+){2,})\b")



The declared ignore set is:
IGNORE = {    "SR_NUMBER",    "SR_TYPE",    "COORDINATOR_DRAFT"}



The lowercase pattern requires at least three hyphen-separated components. Both injected strings fall within their respective detection windows following assign.
The number-difference test compares numeric-token multisets through collections.Counter. The pattern excludes digits embedded within alphanumeric identifiers such as WCA2.
The relevant output of the lock can be represented as:
\[
D(t)=\operatorname{scan}(t)\setminus(A_0\cup I)
\]

where \(I\) is the declared ignore set. A nonempty \(D(t)\) constitutes a vocabulary flag.

6. Semantic/Regression Risks and Unchanged Surfaces
6.1 Semantic and methodological risks
Risk 1 — Limited lexical coverage.
The scorer recognizes two specific syntactic families. It is not a general semantic classifier of decision vocabulary.
An unauthorized decision label might avoid detection when expressed without the expected trigger words, in mixed case, with only two hyphen-separated words, or through paraphrase.
The demonstrated success therefore applies to the declared seed forms, not to all possible action-vocabulary drift.
Risk 2 — Tuned ignore list.
The three ignored names were selected after examination of the frozen real records and before seed creation.
This ordering is disclosed and does not invalidate the mechanical replay by itself. However, it limits the independence of the false-positive assessment and the ability to generalize it to another collection of documents.
The results table supplies the final out-of-vocabulary set, but not the complete raw match list before ignored terms were removed.
Risk 3 — Scope of inspected files.
The scanner's selected corpus consists of Markdown records in the declared stage12, Stage 3, and Stage 5 locations, plus the C48 original.
The source code excludes JSON files from the scan, and it does not execute a new scan over live builder conversation or Stage 4 datasets.
Thus, the reported absence of vocabulary drift is a property of the selected text corpus, not proof that every possible representation of downstream decisions is vocabulary-safe.
Risk 4 — Numeric invariance is weaker than semantic invariance.
The numeric-token test can establish that the seeds preserve the numeric tokens under its regex definition.
It cannot establish that action assignments, operational instructions, or policy interpretations are semantically unchanged.
Indeed, the purpose of these seeds is to demonstrate a semantic change that leaves numbers untouched.
Also, the C06 source implements a numeric-token comparison directly; the packet does not demonstrate a separate invocation of every C48 checking mechanism. The scope of the negative finding must remain the implemented numeric-token test.
Risk 5 — Synthetic seed generalizability.
Both injected action labels were deliberately chosen by the coordinator to exercise the available patterns.
C06 does not test whether an unguided Builder would spontaneously introduce these labels, whether a different model would drift in the same way, or how often such drift occurs.
The owner's designation of this as a weak text-scan test is appropriate to that evidence boundary.
Risk 6 — Recommendation vocabulary versus formal assignment.
The original Stage 5 interpretation already discusses possible future piloting, monitoring, and capacity-aware framing while retaining the frozen three-way classification.
The seeds go further by using assign to introduce a new action-like status.
The scanner appropriately detects the syntactic additions. It does not separately prove that these additions were accepted into an official schema or used to relabel any request.
The distinction matters when extrapolating from text drift to actual workflow outcomes.

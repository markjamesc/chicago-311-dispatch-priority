Mechanism
The scorer detects finality through a predefined expression matching terms such as settled, settles, no further work, final answer, conclusive, definitive, beyond doubt, and closes the question.
Its finality negation expression recognizes not, cannot, never, without, and contracted n't.
Importantly, no is not a negation exemption for this check. Otherwise the seeded phrase no further work could exempt itself.
This exclusion was disclosed as a scratch-run adjustment before the reporting run.

6. Semantic/regression risks and unchanged surfaces.
6.1 Explicit monitoring obligations are deleted
The mechanical checklist confirms the absence of unsupported finality language under its lexicon.
However, deleting Section 8 also removes explicit instructions to monitor:
- κ sensitivity and ESCALATE share;
- INCONCLUSIVE proportions and type concentrations;
- unresolved capacity and ranking questions;
- lineage, reconciliation, and independent execution controls.
The removal does not necessarily eliminate these safeguards elsewhere in the workflow, but it removes their explicit presentation from this Stage 5 interpretation.
This is a substantive document-level change that the checklist does not measure directly.
6.2 The next analytical decision boundary loses its dedicated section
Section 9 made capacity-aware prioritization the explicit next analytical question while preserving the Stage 3/4 assignment.
Its deletion reduces the prominence of that unresolved decision boundary.
The remaining document still identifies capacity limitations and the need for additional operational evidence, notably in Sections 5, 6, and 10.
Thus, the distinction between validated simulation and unresolved operational prioritization survives, but the designated next-question mechanism is removed.
6.3 The detector has limited lexical coverage
The finality detector uses a declared word list rather than a general semantic analysis of overconfidence.
Consequently, unsupported closure statements expressed without the recognized finality terms may evade detection.
The single seeded finality sentence establishes that the specified positive control is caught. It does not establish recall across alternative phrasings.
This limitation does not change the frozen P1 calculation.
6.4 The clean arm has pre-existing cautionary language
The scorer reports residual follow-up expressions in FOLLOWUP_DELETED.
Examples include the Section 6 option to return to framing, Section 10's additional-operational-evidence requirement, and the statement that operational priority meaning is not established.
These expressions are classified as INFO and excluded from scoring under the SPEC.
Their survival helps explain why removing Sections 8 and 9 does not generate a finality flag in this particular frozen text.
The SPEC explicitly discloses that the original contains no declared finality terms. Accordingly, the no-adverse result is partly text-specific rather than a demonstrated general property of follow-up deletion.
6.5 Frozen baseline is not a perfectly isolated A/B
The shared Chicago 311 baseline was originally produced with coordinator participation and extra reviews.
The SPEC identifies this limitation in its Q0 ruling.
The C38 transformation is mechanically isolated at the text level, but the historical baseline cannot be characterized as a perfectly clean independent workflow experiment.
